After [last years first episode](__GHOST_URL__/2012/10/28/contrail-fun-1430-on-sunday-oct-28th-2012/) with contrails, another one popped up today.  During a late afternoon walk, on a sunny Winter’s day, I saw a great contrail right over the Melbourne area:

 

[![20130630_160322](/images/wordpress/2021/02/e7763-20130630_160322_thumb.jpg)](https://dwids.wordpress.com2020/03/6d458-20130630_160322.jpg)

[click on all pics to zoom in]

The FlightRadar24 app on the phone said NZ to South Africa and at about 34,000 ft (~ 10,000 m). So I continued the walk and a bit later I looked left (~West) and the day was so clear, I could still see it ‘miles’ away: I mean it making fresh trails. I firstly snapped a – zoomed in – picture:

[![20130630_161430](/images/wordpress/2021/02/680a6-20130630_161430_thumb.jpg)](https://dwids.wordpress.com2020/03/912d0-20130630_161430.jpg)

Then I checked FlightRadar24 again. It was way over near Skipton:

[![Screenshot_2013-06-30-16-14-54](/images/wordpress/2021/02/5e776-screenshot_2013-06-30-16-14-54_thumb.png)](https://dwids.wordpress.com2020/03/2eb8e-screenshot_2013-06-30-16-14-54.png)

I guessed its angle above the horizon, using the cool [outstretched hand-fist](http://darkskydiary.wordpress.com/2010/03/29/how-big-is-a-degree/) rule. I’d estimated between 5 and 10 degrees.

So later on at home a bit of high-school maths. And some help from Google as I’d forgotten the basics! :

**Skipton is about 130 km from me. The plane is 10 km off the ground. What is the angle (theta) from the horizon?**

So, Tan(theta) = 10/130  [“opposite over adjacent”]

Tan(theta) = 0.0769

theta = 4.4 degrees

Very close to my 5 to 10 degrees. Yay me.
