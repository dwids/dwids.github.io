This was around October 1967

We were from Melbourne, but lived in Adelaide. My late father (Brian) still worked in Melbourne and drove back and forth every few weeks.

On one trip back from Melbourne, he rolled the FJ Holden just outside of **Dimboola**. He’s passed away now, but my understanding is he injured his pelvis and needed an extended stay in the local hospital there.

My Mum (who is now 85) was advised via a visit from the police, over in suburban Adelaide. She had 3 kids; six and under.  I was the eldest at 6.

My Mum is Marian Sidwell, but back then she was Mari**o**n; the difference being a late-life discovery of a ‘typo’ on her early paperwork.

She left the three of us with local friends - the Fry family - and jumped on the overnight train from Adelaide to Melbourne.

It arrived at Dimboola before dawn and the station staff were very helpful; putting her in a warm room and giving her tea etc. When the hospital was open, she visited Dad.

In the meantime my Mum’s younger sister drove up from Melbourne to Dimboola (300 km, one way).  She picked Mum up, in her little VW Bug, and drove her back to Adelaide (400 km, one way).  Mum was apparently back home about a day after leaving.

We packed what we could into the VW and all five of us drove back to Dimboola.

Moving quickly, I understand 'Mick' Haby (from the caravan park?) arranged a house and furniture for us. 

Mum says the house was actually owned by Mr Haby and was in Faith St. His daughter, Pat, lived next door, with her then-husband Charlie Hurnall. They – and their 3 kids - were so helpful to us. I am happy to say that I am back in occasional contact with Trish (Pat), via Mum's Facebook account.

I only have two memories of this:

* Waving at a hospital window where Dad was (apparently) waving back
* Looking out through the front window of the house and seeing a truck or ute pull up with furniture.

I thought we stayed there for many months, but apparently it was ‘only’ about 6 weeks and Dad was out of hospital. I only found out recently, that his late brother — Uncle Derek — drove a truck from Melbourne to Adelaide (~700 km) to load up our house stuff.  Then he drove it back to our new place at Daylesford, Victoria (650 km). By luck, Mum's parents still owned their old Daylesford house — as a rental (they lived in Melbourne) — and the tenants had just moved out.  Hence, my interest in [[daylesford-x3 | Daylesford]]. 

As for me, it's nearly 60 years on and I can still see that vehicle pulling up with the furniture at Dimboola. I’m eternally grateful for the kindness and typical bush care provided to we total strangers.
